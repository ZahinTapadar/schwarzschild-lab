A Journey to the Pillars of Creation

Three columns of cold gas, each light-years tall, stand thousands of light-years from Earth. Starlight is eating them away, and new stars are forming inside them while they burn. This essay flies you there and works out what you are looking at.

On 1 April 1995 Jeff Hester and Paul Scowen pointed the Hubble Space Telescope at a small patch of the Eagle Nebula and exposed its camera through three narrow filters. The picture they assembled became one of the most reproduced photographs in the history of science. It shows three dark fingers rising out of a glowing fog, their tips fringed with light and dotted with small knots that look like droplets about to fall.

Why are the pillars dark when everything around them glows? Why do they all point the same way? Why does Webb see straight through gas that Hubble could not? How big are they, how old, and could you ever go there? By the end you will have worked out every answer yourself.
