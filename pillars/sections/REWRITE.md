# Rewrite brief (round 2). Overrides CONTRACT.md where they differ.

The reader's verdict on round 1: the concepts are good, but the essay is too long, and almost every demo looks static and amateurish ("just sliders", "graphs look horrid"). Only two demos were praised: `fig-journey-flight` (self-playing animation, motion, live HUD, ship-time vs Earth-time bars) and `fig-where-sky` (the animated toy sky). Ideas the reader specifically liked and wants kept: the three pillars one behind another in 3D, "inside the scale", the light-travel photons, the interstellar headwind.

## Rules for round 2
1. **Shorter.** Your section must be at most 1,600 words of prose (about half of round 1). Cut repetition, keep the strongest explanations and the physics. Keep the sources list.
2. **At most 3 interactive figures per section.** Each must be a self-playing animation that looks beautiful when nobody touches it: continuous motion, particles, glow, camera movement, and a live HUD with physical readouts (use `P.hud`). Controls only steer a running scene. No static charts, no plain line graphs, no slider-only diagrams. If a quantity needs a chart, make it animated and part of a scene (a readout, or a bar that fills as the scene plays).
3. **Use real 3D and your own shaders.** Three.js r128 (global `THREE`) and GSAP 3.12 (global `gsap`) are loaded on the page. Use `P.demo3d(id, setup, {aspect})` from `lib/stage3d.js` (read it): it gives you one shared WebGL renderer, so never create your own `WebGLRenderer` or WebGL context. Use `THREE.Points` with a custom `ShaderMaterial` and additive blending for particles, `P.dotTexture()` for soft sprites, `P.orbit(ctx, camera, {...})` for drag-to-orbit with inertia and auto-spin, and `renderTo(r, pw, ph)` for multi-pass work (e.g. ping-pong `THREE.WebGLRenderTarget` simulations; finish with `r.setRenderTarget(null)` and re-apply `r.setViewport(0,0,pw,ph)` and `r.setScissor(0,0,pw,ph)` before the final pass). 2D canvas stays fine for crisp overlays (`overlay(g, w, h, t)`) and for the two praised demos.
4. **The shared volumetric pillars.** `lib/pillars-volume.js` gives `P.pillarsVolume()`: a ray-marched model of the three pillars (1 unit = 1 pc; P1 left/back, P2 middle, P3 front-right; cluster at `uniforms.uCluster`). Uniforms: `uIR` (0 visible look → 1 Webb look), `uKr` (A_λ/A_V dust opacity ratio), `uDust`, `uSkin`, `uStars`, `uAmbient`, `uExposure`, `uSteps`. Render with `V.renderTo(r, perspectiveCamera, t, pw, ph)`. See `sections/00-intro.js` for a working example. Reuse it wherever a section shows the pillars themselves. Do not edit `lib/`; if you need a variant, copy the shader into your own file. Ray-marched scenes should return `scale: 0.6–0.8` to keep the frame rate up.
5. Performance: about 50 fps on a laptop. Particle counts ≤ 60k, raymarch steps ≤ 120. The runtime already stops animation offscreen.
6. Visual language: dark scenes with physically meaningful colour (Hα red, [O III] teal, [S II] amber, blackbody colours for stars, IR purple), soft additive glow, gentle camera drift, smooth easing (GSAP or your own). HUD top-left. Scenes keep their own dark background so they also sit well in a light-themed page; text outside the canvas uses tokens.
7. Delete demos and prose you no longer use. Keep the file names, the `<h2 id>`, the figure-id prefix and the closing bridge sentence. Images: keep only real agency photos that do real work (at most 2 per section; a swipe counts as 1).
8. Physics stays exactly as sound as round 1: same canonical numbers, formulas in code comments, toy models labelled in one short caption sentence.
